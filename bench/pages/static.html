Why idle browsers still drain batteries

By A. Writer · 6 min read

A web page that has finished loading should cost almost nothing to keep on screen. In practice, the browser still runs timers, paints when something changes, and keeps its processes awake. Each of these is small, and together they decide how long a laptop lasts on battery.

This page has no scripts, no animations and no video. It measures the floor: what a browser costs when it only has to keep a finished page visible.

Timers

Pages schedule work with timers. A timer that fires every few milliseconds keeps a processor core from entering its deepest idle state, and every wake-up costs energy before any useful work happens.

Painting

A browser paints a new frame whenever something on screen changes. On a 120 Hz display, an animation that never stops means 120 frames a second, each with its own work on the processor and the graphics chip.

Processes

Modern browsers split pages across many processes. Each process has its own housekeeping, and each one that wakes up costs a little energy even when the page inside it is doing nothing.
